The three ways a hot desk is priced

Almost every coworking space in the Winelands prices flexible desk access in one of three ways. Understanding which one fits your rhythm is where most of the money is saved or wasted.

By the hour. Pay only for the time you are actually at a desk — around R60/hour at The Village Hub. This is ideal for the freelancer who needs three focused hours between meetings, or someone passing through town for a morning. Cheap for short bursts, expensive if you settle in for a full day.

By the day. A day pass — roughly R250 — buys unlimited access for a full working day: arrive at 08:00, leave at 18:00, one flat fee. Best for the person who comes in a couple of days a week, or a remote worker escaping a noisy house before a big deadline.

By the month. An unlimited monthly membership — about R3,000 at The Village Hub — gives you a hot desk every working day with no per-visit thinking. This is the tier that quietly wins for anyone building a routine, because the effective cost per day drops the more you use it.

Day pass vs monthly: the break-even maths

Here is the single calculation that saves the most money. A day pass at R250 sounds flexible and cheap — until you count the days. Twelve day passes cost R3,000, which is exactly the price of an unlimited month. So the break-even is simple:

Fewer than 12 days a month → buy day passes. You keep the flexibility and pay only for what you use.

Twelve days or more → buy the monthly membership. Every day beyond the twelfth is effectively free, your per-day cost keeps falling, and you stop paying at the door each morning. Most people who tell themselves they will "just use day passes" end up coming in fifteen or sixteen days a month — and overpay by hundreds of rand before they notice.

The hourly rate has its own break-even against the day pass: beyond about four hours in a day, a R250 day pass is cheaper than paying R60/hour. If you routinely stay longer than half a day, stop buying hours and buy the day.

What a fair price should include

A hot-desk rate is only meaningful once you know what comes with it. At a well-run space, the number on the pricing page should already cover:

  • Fast, reliable Wi-Fi — business-grade, with a backup line for load-shedding.
  • Free parking — secure and on-site, for you and any guest who visits.
  • Good coffee and common areas — not a vending machine you feed coins into.
  • Guest access — the ability to meet a client in a lounge or breakout area without a surcharge.
  • Power through outages — backup power so a scheduled outage does not end your working day.

If those are included, a R250 day pass or R3,000 month is doing real work. If they are billed as extras, the headline rate is a teaser and your true cost is higher.

The hidden costs to check first

Before you commit anywhere, ask five questions. Each one exposes a cost that rarely makes the pricing page:

1. Is parking really free, or paid per day? In central Stellenbosch, paid street parking or a parkade can add R40–R60 a day — up to R1,200 a month on top of your desk.

2. What does a guest cost? Some spaces charge for visitors or for the meeting room you need to host them. If you meet clients, that adds up fast.

3. Is there a minimum term or joining fee? A monthly membership should be month-to-month. Watch for setup fees or lock-ins dressed up as a discount.

4. Are meeting rooms extra? Usually yes — and fairly so — but check the hourly rate (from R180/hour is typical) so a big client day does not surprise you.

5. What about after-hours and weekends? If your best work happens after 17:00, confirm that access is included rather than a premium add-on.
