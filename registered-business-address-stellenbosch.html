Registered Business Address in Stellenbosch

What CIPC actually requires, what SARS and your bank will ask for on top, what it costs in 2026 — and why the cheapest address on the internet is usually the one that causes you problems.

Last updated 15 September 2026 · 8-minute read

Every company registered in South Africa must have a registered office address. It is not a formality you can improvise: it is the address where the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC), SARS, the courts and the sheriff will look for you, and it appears on your public company record. Get it wrong and the consequences are slow but real — a summons you never see, a VAT registration that stalls, a bank account application that goes quiet.

What a registered address legally is

Under the Companies Act, every company and close corporation must maintain a registered office in South Africa. The point of it is service: it is the place where legal documents can be physically delivered to your company and be deemed to have reached you. That single purpose explains almost every rule that follows.

In practice it means three things. It must be a physical street address — a building someone can walk into, not a post box. It must be in South Africa — a foreign address will not do, even if your directors live abroad. And it must be an address where you can actually be reached, because service at your registered address counts as service on you whether or not the document ever reaches a human who cares.

The last point is the one that catches people. If a summons is served at an address you stopped visiting eighteen months ago, the court does not especially mind. Default judgment can follow against a company that genuinely never knew it was being sued. A registered address is not a branding exercise; it is your legal letterbox.

The three addresses nobody tells you about

Most founders assume there is one company address. There are three, they are held by different institutions, and updating one does not update the others. This is the single most common compliance gap we see.

1. Your CIPC registered office. Held on the public company register, changed by filing a notice of change of registered office with CIPC. This is the address a creditor or a competitor can look up.

2. Your SARS registered address. Held separately on eFiling. SARS uses it for correspondence, verification and — critically — for VAT and PAYE registration checks. SARS has become materially more rigorous about verifying that a business address is real, and a mismatch between your CIPC address and your SARS address is a common reason a registration sits in limbo.

3. Your bank's FICA address. Banks must verify your business address under FICA. They will typically want a signed lease or membership agreement plus a recent utility bill or invoice tied to the premises. A letter saying you may use an address is usually not enough on its own.

Change premises and you have three updates to make, not one. Put a reminder in the diary for all three on the day you move.

Your four options in Stellenbosch

Option 1: Your home address

Free, and perfectly legal for most small companies. The problems are practical rather than technical. Your home address becomes publicly searchable on the CIPC register. Clients see a residential street on your invoices and quotes. If you move house, you have three registrations to update. And if a sheriff arrives, he arrives where your family lives. Fine for a first year; most founders outgrow it.

Option 2: Your accountant's or attorney's address

Common, and reasonable if they agree in writing and genuinely handle your post. The risk is that the relationship is the address. When you change accountants — and most businesses eventually do — you have to move your registered office again, and the outgoing firm has little incentive to forward anything. It also tells SARS that no business activity happens at your registered address, which is unhelpful if you are applying for VAT.

Option 3: A standalone virtual or mailbox address

The cheapest option advertised, often a few hundred rand a month. You get an address and post handling, and nothing else. It is legal on its face, and for a dormant holding company it can be entirely appropriate. For a trading business it is the option most likely to cause you trouble — see the next section.

Option 4: A workspace you actually hold

A desk membership or private office at a coworking space. You get a commercial street address, a signed agreement that proves a right to occupy, reception that receives your post, and somewhere to put a person when a bank, SARS or a client wants to see one. It costs more than a mailbox and less than a conventional lease, and it is the only one of the four where the address and the business activity are in the same place.

Why mailbox addresses go wrong

Nothing in the Companies Act forbids using a service address. The difficulty arises downstream, with the institutions that have to believe your address is real.

SARS verification. VAT registration in particular attracts scrutiny, because address-only shell registrations have been used for fraud. SARS may ask for proof of business premises and may verify it. An address you have never visited, with no occupancy agreement behind it, is the weakest possible answer.

Bank onboarding. FICA requires the bank to verify the address. A mailbox provider's consent letter frequently fails this test where a signed lease or membership agreement passes it.

Shared-address flagging. When hundreds of unrelated companies share one address, that address acquires a reputation. Automated risk systems at banks, insurers and credit bureaus notice. You inherit the reputation of every other company at your number.

Tender and client due diligence. Corporate procurement and government tenders increasingly check whether the registered address resolves to a real place of business. A mailbox can cost you a bid.

The honest summary: a mailbox address is a saving of a few hundred rand a month that buys you a small but persistent risk in every institutional relationship you have. For a dormant entity, take the saving. For a trading business that wants credit, VAT and corporate clients, do not.

How to change your registered address

The mechanics are straightforward once you know there are several steps rather than one.

Step 1 — Secure the new address. Get the signed agreement first, whether that is a lease or a workspace membership. You will need it as supporting evidence more than once.

Step 2 — File the change with CIPC. Lodge the notice of change of registered office (historically form CoR21.1) through CIPC e-services. The change takes effect when CIPC records it, or on a later date you specify in the notice.

Step 3 — Update SARS on eFiling. Separately, under your registered details. Do not assume the CIPC filing carries across, because it does not.

Step 4 — Update your bank. Send the signed agreement and whatever proof of address the bank's FICA team asks for.

Step 5 — Update everything customer-facing. Invoice templates, quotes, email signatures, your website footer, your Google Business Profile and your letterheads. An invoice showing an address that no longer matches your CIPC record is an easy thing for a client's finance team to query and a slow thing to fix.

This is general information on how the process works, not legal or tax advice. If your situation involves a dispute, a VAT application under review, or a company in distress, take advice from your attorney or accountant before you move anything.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use a coworking space as my registered business address in Stellenbosch?

Yes, provided the arrangement is genuine. CIPC requires a physical street address in South Africa where the company can be served. A coworking space where you hold a desk or office membership, receive post and can actually be found satisfies that comfortably. What does not work is buying an address you never visit and have no contractual right to occupy.

Can I use a PO Box as a registered address?

No. The registered office must be a physical street address where documents can be served in person. You may list a PO Box separately as a postal address, but it cannot be the registered office.

Will SARS accept a coworking address for VAT registration?

SARS has tightened verification of business addresses and generally wants evidence that real activity happens there. A signed membership or lease agreement, invoices in the company name, and the ability to receive a physical visit all help. An address with no occupancy rights behind it is the kind most likely to be rejected.

Does changing my CIPC address change it at SARS too?

No. CIPC, SARS and your bank each hold the address separately and each must be updated on its own. This is the step most often missed, and the reason correspondence goes to an address a business left years ago.

What happens if my registered address is out of date?

Legal documents served at the registered address are generally treated as having reached the company. That means a summons you never saw can still lead to default judgment, and SARS correspondence you never received still runs its clock. It is a quiet risk until the day it is not.

Do I need to be at the office every day to use it as my address?

No. What matters is that the arrangement is real: a signed agreement, a genuine right to use the space, and post that reaches you. A hot-desk member who comes in twice a week is on entirely solid ground.
