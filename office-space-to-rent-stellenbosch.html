Why per-square-metre and per-month don't compare

Conventional commercial office space in South Africa is quoted as a base rental per square metre per month, excluding VAT. On top of that sits an operating cost or levy - rates, common-area maintenance, security, sometimes water. Then come the items that are entirely yours: electricity, fibre, cleaning, consumables, furniture, fit-out, insurance and a deposit.

A serviced or private office is quoted as one figure per office per month. Furniture, fibre, cleaning, utilities, security, reception and shared meeting rooms are already inside it.

So a R110/m² listing and a R13,950/month office aren't two prices for the same thing. One is roughly 55–70% of your eventual monthly cost; the other is close to 100% of it. Every honest comparison starts by loading the first number up until it covers what the second already includes.

The true-cost checklist for a conventional lease

Before you compare anything, price these eleven line items for the conventional option. Most tenants underestimate the total by a third.

  • Base rental - rate per m² × lettable area, plus annual escalation (commonly 7–9%).
  • Operating costs / levies - often 20–35% on top of base rental.
  • VAT - 15%, and only recoverable if you're VAT registered.
  • Electricity - your own account, your own load-shedding contingency.
  • Fibre and hardware - installation, router, monthly line, and a backup line if uptime matters.
  • Fit-out - partitioning, flooring, lighting, data cabling, aircon. A once-off that rarely comes back to you.
  • Furniture - desks, chairs, storage, a meeting table. Budget realistically per seat.
  • Cleaning and consumables - weekly service plus coffee, milk, paper.
  • Meeting space - a boardroom you use six hours a week still costs rent 168 hours a week.
  • Reception and access control - who lets couriers in, and who holds the keys.
  • Deposit and suretyship - typically one to three months up front, and frequently a personal surety from a director.

That last item is the one people notice too late. A five-year lease with a personal suretyship is a five-year personal liability, not a five-year business expense.

Where the office stock sits around Stellenbosch

Stellenbosch office space clusters in four broad places, and they suit different businesses.

  • Central Stellenbosch / Dorp Street area. Character buildings, walkable to everything, strongest for client-facing professional firms. The trade-off is parking - in university term time it is genuinely difficult, and staff and visitors feel it daily.
  • Techno Park. The established business and tech park. Purpose-built stock, good access to the R44, and where much of Stellenbosch's tech sector sits. Mostly multi-year leases with tenant fit-out; small furnished units are rare.
  • Plankenbrug and the industrial belt. Where warehouse and light-industrial space with attached offices sits. Right answer if you need storage or workshop space attached to your desks.
  • Edge-of-town estates - Nooitgedacht, Klapmuts corridor, the R304 side. Newer buildings, generous free parking, quick access toward Paarl, Klapmuts and the N1, and about ten minutes from the town centre. This is where most of the flexible and serviced office stock has appeared, and it is the practical answer for teams whose staff commute in by car from Somerset West, Durbanville, Paarl or Brackenfell.

If you are weighing central against edge-of-town, rand per square metre is not the only factor: the parking question alone changes the daily experience of the office. How much space you actually need

The conventional planning rule is roughly 8–12 m² per person once you allow for circulation, a meeting room, a kitchen and storage. Four people therefore lands somewhere near 40–50 m², and eight people near 80–100 m².

In a serviced office you buy seats, not square metres, because the kitchen, lounges, boardroom and breakout space sit outside your door and are shared. Practically, a 4-seater private office plus bookable boardroom access does the work of 45–60 m² of conventional space - you simply stop paying rent on the rooms you use for four hours a week.

One warning worth repeating: don't size for the team you hope to have in eighteen months. Size for now, and rent somewhere you can add an office when the hiring actually happens. Paying for empty desks is the most common and least visible waste in a small company's overheads.

Lease terms worth negotiating - or avoiding

If you do go the conventional route, these are the clauses that matter most to a small or growing business:

  • Escalation. A 9% annual escalation on a five-year lease raises your rent by more than 40% by year five. Push for 6–7% and make sure it applies to base rental only, not to operating costs as well.
  • A break clause. Even a single break at month 24, on notice, transforms the risk profile of the deal. Ask for it early - it is far cheaper to negotiate before signature than to buy out afterwards.
  • Beneficial occupation. Rent-free time during fit-out, in writing, with dates.
  • What the landlord installs. Get the fit-out split explicit: who pays for partitioning, aircon, data cabling, lighting - and who owns it at the end.
  • Reinstatement. Many leases require you to return the space to its original condition. That is a real cost at the end of the term; know it at the start.
  • Suretyship. Try to cap it in both amount and time rather than signing an unlimited personal guarantee.

None of that is exotic - it is standard commercial practice - but it is only available to you before you sign.

Frequently asked questions

How much does office space cost to rent in Stellenbosch in 2026?

Conventional space is quoted per square metre per month excluding VAT, plus an operating cost, and excludes electricity, fibre, cleaning and furniture. Private offices at The Village Hub are one all-in monthly figure: from R6,950 for a 2-seater, R13,950 for a 4-seater, R20,950 for a 6-seater and R28,000 for an 8-seater.

What's the difference between a conventional lease and a serviced office?

A conventional lease is typically three to five years, priced per square metre, with fit-out, furniture, fibre, electricity, cleaning and a boardroom all at your cost - plus a deposit and often a personal suretyship. A serviced office is a single monthly amount, furnished and connected, on a much shorter commitment, with meeting rooms shared and paid by the hour.

Is there office space to rent in Techno Park?

Techno Park is Stellenbosch's main business park, and most stock there is conventional space on multi-year leases with tenant fit-out. Small furnished units are scarce. If you want a ready-to-occupy office without a long lease in the same catchment, the edge-of-town flexible offices - including ours at Nooitgedacht Village, ten minutes out - are the usual alternative, with free parking included.

How much office space do I need per person?

Roughly 8–12 m² per person for conventional space once circulation, meeting space and a kitchen are allowed for. In a serviced office you buy seats instead, and shared space sits outside your office - so a 4-seater plus boardroom access typically replaces 45–60 m².

Can I start smaller and grow into a bigger office?
