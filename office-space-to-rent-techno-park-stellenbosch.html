The six clauses that cost people money

We broker commercial space across the Cape Town metro and the Winelands, and the same six clauses account for most of the unpleasant surprises we see.

1. The escalation, and how it compounds

An escalation clause compounds. Over a five-year term the difference between a 7% and a 9% annual escalation is not two percent — it is roughly a tenth of your total rental commitment. Ask for the rand figure in year five, in writing, before you sign year one.

2. What the operating cost recovery actually recovers

Operating cost is the single most variable line in a commercial lease. Ask for the current year's schedule and last year's actuals. If the landlord cannot produce them, that is itself the answer.

3. Parking, in bays and in rands

Parking is where Stellenbosch quietly punishes you. In-town premises often come with a handful of bays for a team of twelve, and the rest of your staff pay for public parking daily or walk in from wherever they found a spot. Count heads, count bays, and price the gap. On a team of ten this line alone can run to several thousand rand a month and it never appears in the headline rate.

4. Who pays for the fit-out

A shell needs partitioning, lighting, data cabling, air conditioning and furniture before anyone can work in it. That is real capital, spent before you earn a cent from the space. Ask whether there is a tenant installation allowance, and whether it is a contribution or a rent-free period — they are not the same thing on your cash flow.

5. The reinstatement clause

This is the one that catches first-time tenants hardest. Many leases require you to return the premises to their original condition at your cost — which can mean stripping out the fit-out you paid to install. Read it, price it, and negotiate it at the start, when you still have leverage.

6. Notice, and what happens if you grow

Growing companies misjudge this constantly. A five-year lease signed at eight people is a problem at eighteen. Ask about expansion rights within the building, whether you may sublet, and what the early-termination position is. If the honest answer is "none, no and punitive", size the space for where you will be in three years, not where you are today.

The honest summary: if you are a stable team of fifteen or more and you know where you will be in four years, a conventional lease in Techno Park will beat a serviced office on rate per person. If you are a team of two to twelve, or you are growing, or you simply do not want to spend a quarter of the year buying desks and arguing with a fibre installer, the serviced route wins on both cash and attention.

Where the break-even actually sits

Do the comparison properly and it is not close at small headcounts. Build the conventional number as: base rental, plus operating cost, plus rates, plus utilities, plus the extra parking bays, plus fit-out amortised over the term, plus internet, plus cleaning, plus the hours your operations person spends running the building. Then compare that monthly total to the serviced figure.

For most teams under about ten desks the serviced office is cheaper in total cost and dramatically cheaper in cash up front. Somewhere between ten and twelve desks the two cross. Above fifteen, conventional space usually wins — and at that point the fit-out becomes an asset you are happy to own.

Questions we get asked

Where exactly is Techno Park?

Off the R44 on the southern edge of Stellenbosch. It is the town's established technology and business park and the default search for professional office space here. Nooitgedacht Village, where we are, is about fifteen minutes north on the R304.

Is a serviced office really cheaper?

Below roughly ten to twelve desks, yes — on total monthly cost, and by a wide margin on cash up front. Above fifteen desks a conventional lease usually wins on rate per person. Run your own numbers using the line items in the table above rather than taking anyone's word for it, ours included.

That is the main reason teams choose this route. Start on desks, move into a two-person office, move again into a four or six when you need it — without a lease renegotiation each time. Several of our current office tenants arrived as desk members first.

What about parking?
