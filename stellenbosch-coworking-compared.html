Hello Work vs Regus vs The Village Hub: Stellenbosch Coworking Compared

Three genuinely different models compete for your desk in Stellenbosch. Here's an honest comparison — including where our competitors beat us.

Last updated 20 July 2026 · 9-minute read

Stellenbosch has quietly become one of the Western Cape's strongest coworking towns. Between the university, the tech and agri-business cluster, the wine industry and a fast-growing remote-work population, demand for flexible workspace here keeps climbing — and three very different answers to that demand now compete for your desk: an independent creative space, a global serviced-office giant, and a lifestyle-estate hub.

Full disclosure up front: we run The Village Hub at Nooitgedacht Village, so we are not a neutral referee. What we can promise is a fair scorecard. Each of these spaces is genuinely good at something, each suits a different kind of member, and pretending otherwise would insult your intelligence. Here's how they actually compare in mid-2026.

The three contenders at a glance

Hello Work is an independent, creative coworking space at The Woodmill Lifestyle Centre, offering hot desks, dedicated desks, private offices and two meeting rooms with a strong design sensibility and a loyal local following.

Regus is the world's largest serviced-office operator, part of the IWG group, with a Stellenbosch location that plugs you into a global network of lounges and offices — hot desks by the hour or day, dedicated desks and private offices, priced on enquiry.

The Village Hub — that's us — is a premium coworking and boutique-accommodation hub at Nooitgedacht Village on the R44 wine route: 36 hot desks, dedicated desks, private offices, a boardroom and meeting rooms, plus The Village Stay suites upstairs, inside a lifestyle estate with a restaurant, gym and free unlimited parking.

Hello Work — the creative independent

Hello Work has earned its position as one of Stellenbosch's best-known coworking brands. The space at The Woodmill is contemporary and creative, with natural light, fast Wi-Fi and a design-led fit-out that photographs as well as it works. Options run from hot desks and dedicated desks on daily, weekly and monthly terms through to serviced private offices on month-to-month agreements — no long leases, which is exactly how flexible workspace should be sold.

Where it's strong: the creative atmosphere is genuine, not marketing. Private office members get 24/7 access in a secure building, boardroom use, printing and kitchen facilities are bundled in, and The Woodmill location puts you near shops and coffee without being in the town-centre parking crush. For freelancers and small creative teams who want personality in their workspace, it's a strong choice.

What to weigh up: coworking (as opposed to private office) hours run Monday to Friday, 8am to 5pm — fine for most, limiting if you work early, late or weekends on a desk membership. There are two meeting rooms seating up to ten, so peak-time availability can be tight. Pricing for most options is on enquiry rather than published.

Regus — the global network

Regus needs little introduction: it is the biggest name in serviced offices worldwide, and its Stellenbosch location gives the town a corporate-grade flexible workspace option. You can hot-desk by the hour or the day, take a dedicated desk, or move a team into a private office — and higher-tier memberships unlock Regus locations across South Africa and internationally, which no local independent can match.

Where it's strong: if your work has you in Cape Town on Tuesday, Johannesburg on Thursday and London next month, the network is the product. Standardised service, professional reception, and the reassurance of a listed global group behind the agreement make it a safe corporate procurement choice. Booking and account management run through a polished app.

What to weigh up: standardisation cuts both ways — a Regus floor in Stellenbosch feels much like a Regus floor anywhere, and the local community layer is thinner than at the independents. Pricing is quote-based and can move with demand; the advertised entry rates typically attach to longer commitments, so read the term carefully. It's an office product first, a community second.

The Village Hub — the lifestyle-estate hub

Our model is different from both. The Village Hub sits inside Nooitgedacht Village, a lifestyle estate five minutes off the R44 between Stellenbosch and Klapmuts — which means the workspace comes with an estate around it: The Village Table restaurant for coffee and client lunches, a gym across the square, boutique accommodation upstairs at The Village Stay (from R1,350 per night), and free, secure, unlimited parking that never requires a validation ticket.

Where we're strong: transparent published pricing — hot desks at R60 per hour, R250 for a full day, or R3,000 per month for unlimited weekday access; dedicated desks at R3,500; private offices from R6,950; meeting rooms from R180 per hour and a proper boardroom for hire. Booking runs through our member app, so a day pass takes two minutes to arrange. Members and guests park on-site for free, and the estate setting makes hosting clients easy — they park, walk in, and lunch is thirty metres away. Our Google rating stands at 5.0 across our reviews, which we work hard to keep.

What to weigh up: we are a short drive from town rather than in it — around 10 to 15 minutes from central Stellenbosch. If you walk to work from a town-centre flat, that matters. We're also a single location: we can't hand you a lounge in Sandton or Singapore the way Regus can. And as a newer space, our community is growing rather than established — something we consider an opportunity, but a fair-minded comparison names it.

Frequently asked questions

What is the cheapest way to try coworking in Stellenbosch?

A single hot-desk session. At The Village Hub that's R60 for an hour or R250 for a full day with no membership required; Hello Work sells daily hot desks and Regus offers hourly and daily access, both priced on enquiry. Spend a day in each before committing to a monthly plan.

Which Stellenbosch coworking space is best for hosting clients?

If your guests arrive by car — as most Winelands clients do — the estate model is hard to beat: free unlimited guest parking, a boardroom from R180 per hour, and The Village Table for the post-meeting lunch, all in one precinct. If your clients are already in central Stellenbosch on foot, a town-side space may suit better.

Do any of these spaces require long leases?

No — all three sell flexibility. Hello Work works on flexible monthly agreements, Regus on rolling memberships and serviced terms, and The Village Hub month-to-month at R3,000 for unlimited weekday hot-desk access, R3,500 for a dedicated desk, and private offices from R6,950.

Which coworking space in Stellenbosch has the best parking?

Estate and lifestyle-centre spaces win on parking. The Village Hub includes free, secure, unlimited parking for members and guests; Hello Work has parking at The Woodmill. Town-centre spaces generally depend on paid street parking or parkades — read our full parking guide for the numbers.
