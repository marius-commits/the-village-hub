Private Office for a Small Team in Stellenbosch: What 2 to 8 Desks Really Cost

Most quotes you will be given are not comparable. One is a rate per square metre before costs; the other is a single monthly figure with everything in it. Here is how to compare them properly — and what each team size actually pays in Stellenbosch in 2026.

Last updated 12 October 2026 · 8-minute read

The short version

Those figures already include furniture, utilities, Wi-Fi, cleaning, parking and 24/7 access. Why a conventional lease quote is not comparable

If you have asked a commercial agent about office space to let in Stellenbosch, you will have been quoted a rate per square metre per month. Say R135/m² for a decent Techno Park unit. For a four-person team at conventional planning ratios you need somewhere around 40m² once circulation and storage are counted, so the headline comes out near R5,400/month. Against R13,950, that looks like an easy decision.

It is not, because the headline is the start of the invoice rather than the end of it. On a conventional lease you should expect to add:

Add those back and the gap narrows sharply, and in many cases closes entirely. But the number is not the real story. The real story is the last two rows. A five-year lease with a compounding escalation and a surety signed by a director is a material commitment for a team of four, and it is the wrong shape of risk for a business that might double or halve inside eighteen months.

The honest comparison test

Ask any landlord or agent for a fully occupied monthly cost: base rent, operating costs, rates, utilities, internet, cleaning and the monthly cost of amortising furniture and fit-out over the term. Then divide by the number of people you will actually seat. If they cannot give you that figure, you do not yet have a quote — you have an opening position.

Which room size actually fits

Two people: a founder and a first hire

At this stage the thing you are buying is a door. Two people on hot desks will work for a while, but it stops working the day you need to take calls without booking a room every time, or leave two monitors and a filing drawer in place overnight. The Personal Office at R6,950 is the smallest step that solves both, and at two people it is R3,475 each — close to what a dedicated desk costs without the door.

Three to four people: the awkward middle

This is where most teams overspend. The instinct is to take the bigger room because someone is starting next quarter. Since the per-seat cost barely moves, that instinct costs you real money for an empty chair. A better pattern: take the room that fits today and put the incoming person on a dedicated desk at R3,500 or a month pass at R3,000 for their first weeks, then move the team up when the seat is genuinely filled.

Six to eight people: where the lease question returns

Around six people the conventional-lease comparison becomes genuinely competitive on cost, and above eight it often wins outright — if you are willing to carry the fit-out, the admin and the term. Most teams this size who choose serviced space are not doing it to save money. They are doing it because they want one invoice, no facilities management, and the ability to change their mind in a month rather than in 2031. Both are defensible. Just be clear which one you are buying.

A team that only needs a room sometimes

If your people are in the building two days a week, a private office is often the wrong product entirely. A month pass plus booked meeting space will usually land well under an office rate. Our boardroom and meeting room hire runs from R350/hour for the 10-seat Village Chamber and R390/hour for the 12-seat Village Boardroom, which is a cheaper way to buy occasional formality than renting it by the month.

A six-question checklist before you sign anything

  1. What is the fully occupied monthly cost per seat? Not the rent. Everything, divided by people.
  2. What is the shortest term you will accept, and what does leaving early cost? The answer tells you how much optionality you are really being sold.
  3. What happens when we need one more desk? If the answer involves moving buildings, price that move in now.
  4. Is the internet yours or ours? Installation lead times have sunk more move-in dates than any other line item.
  5. What is parking, really? Count bays, not reassurances.
  6. Where do we put twelve people for a morning? If the building has no answer, you will be hiring venues all year.

Is a serviced office cheaper than a conventional lease?

For a team under about ten people it usually is, once operating costs, rates, utilities, internet, furniture, cleaning, insurance and the deposit are added to the lease side. Above that size a conventional lease often wins on cost alone, and the decision becomes one about term risk and administration rather than rand per seat.

How many square metres does a four-person office need?

Conventional planning allows roughly 8–12m² per person including circulation and storage, so 32–48m² for four people. In a serviced office you pay for the room and the seats instead of the measured area, and the shared facilities sit outside your rate rather than inflating it.

Can we grow without moving?

Yes. Teams move up through the 2, 3, 4, 6 and 8-seat rooms as they grow, and can hold overflow people on a dedicated desk or month pass in between. Because agreements are month-based, growth does not mean breaking a lease.
